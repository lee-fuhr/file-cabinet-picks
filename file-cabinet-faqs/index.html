File Cabinet questions answered

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

The best file cabinet for you depends on your space, filing volume, and security needs. Vertical cabinets save floor space, while laterals are easier to access and can double as surfaces. Focus on drawer glide quality, lock reliability, and material durability, not just price.

What types of file cabinets are there?

The three main types are vertical, lateral, and mobile pedestal cabinets. Vertical cabinets are tall and narrow, typically one drawer wide, designed to fit in tight spaces like cubicles or home offices. They use a pull-out drawer system that requires clearance in front. Lateral cabinets are wider, often two or three drawers across, and let you file side-to-side. They can double as a credenza or printer stand, and the drawers are usually shallower, making it easier to see all files at once. Mobile pedestals are rolling units that slide under a desk – great for personal filing but limited in capacity.

What material should I choose: steel or wood?

Steel is the workhorse choice. It’s durable, resistant to fire and water damage, and typically comes with a baked-on enamel finish that holds up to daily use. Steel cabinets are heavier, which adds stability, and they usually have better locking mechanisms. Wood cabinets look warmer and can match your office décor, but they are more prone to scratches, dents, and moisture swelling. Particleboard or MDF models are budget-friendly but may sag over time under heavy file loads. If you choose wood, look for solid wood construction or at least a sturdy laminate veneer over engineered wood.

How do I know which size I need?

First, measure your space: vertical cabinets need about 3 feet in front for the drawer to open, lateral ones need less depth but more width. Then decide between letter-size (for standard 8.5x11 documents) and legal-size (for longer sheets). Many cabinets come with a removable metal bar that lets you switch between the two. Depth is critical – most filing systems use hanging file folders, so the interior must be deep enough to accommodate them without bending. Typical depths are 15 to 18 inches for letter, up to 26 inches for legal. Also check the height: a 2-drawer lateral is often counter-height, while a 4-drawer vertical can tower over a desk.

What should I look for in drawer glides and locking?

Drawer glides determine how smoothly the drawer opens and whether it can support a full load. Ball-bearing glides are the gold standard: they run on rollers, handle heavy files, and extend the drawer fully so you can reach the back. Friction glides are cheaper but sticky under weight and rarely offer full extension. For locking, a central lock that secures all drawers with one key is standard. Some cabinets have a separate lock for each drawer – useful for shared offices. The lock itself should be a pin-tumbler type for basic security; if you handle sensitive documents, look for a high-security disc or side-bar lock that is harder to pick.

Are fire-resistant file cabinets worth it?

Fire-resistant (or fireproof) cabinets use insulation and a special seal to keep interior temperatures below the ignition point of paper for a certain time – usually 30, 60, or 120 minutes. They are worth it if you store deeds, contracts, or other irreplaceable documents that cannot be backed up digitally. These cabinets are significantly heavier and bulkier, often twice the weight of a standard steel cabinet. They also cost a few hundred dollars more, so they’re not necessary for everyday office records that you could re-create or that exist in digital form. For most home offices, a solid steel cabinet with a good lock is sufficient.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a lateral file cabinet as a surface for a printer or monitor?

Many lateral file cabinets are rated to support a few hundred pounds on top, making them fine for a printer, monitor, or even a small plant. But check the manufacturer’s weight rating – some budget models have thin tops that can warp under constant weight.

What is the difference between a file cabinet lock and a high-security lock?

A basic file cabinet lock uses a standard pin-tumbler or wafer mechanism that can be picked with basic tools. A high-security lock uses a restricted keyway, hardened steel pins, and often a disc or sidebar design. If your documents are sensitive, choose high security – but it will require a locksmith to cut replacement keys.

How much weight can a file cabinet drawer hold?

Weight capacity depends on the drawer suspension. Ball-bearing glides can typically handle 50 to 100 pounds per drawer, while friction glides may top out around 30 to 40 pounds. Overloading a drawer can bend the glide or cause the cabinet to tip, so always stay within the rated limit printed in the manual.

Do I need a file cabinet that accommodates hanging file folders?

Almost all modern file cabinets are designed for hanging file folders, but some very basic or vintage models may only accept manila folders stored upright. Hanging files are the norm because they keep papers neat and slides easily. When shopping, confirm that the cabinet includes the hanging rod or that the drawers have slots to install your own.

Where is the best place to buy a file cabinet?

Office supply stores (like Staples or Office Depot) carry a wide range of vertical and lateral models you can test in person. Online retailers often have more options and competitive pricing, but shipping heavy cabinets can be expensive. Whichever source you use, check return policies – a file cabinet is a bulky item to send back.

In shortChoosing a file cabinet boils down to three questions: how much floor space you have, how many files you need to store, and what level of security or fire protection your documents require. Spend your money on smooth ball-bearing glides and a reliable lock – those two features will determine your happiness with the cabinet far more than the brand name or finish.
